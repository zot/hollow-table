# Dockets

A named bundle of paper on the table: the label tied round a bundle of papers, its papers inside red
tape. From `carves/done/organizing.md`, Item 4 (Bill, 2026-11-24: "Pin to a docket").

## The docket

- **A docket is a label** taken from its own pigeonhole on the desk, like a sticky (see `table.md`, *The
  bar*): a small tag of card with its name written on it in the hand font, as a pool's name is. A new
  one is named "Docket", the word selected, so typing names it; clicking the name writes it again.
- **It is paper.** It records its maker and who reads it, folded corner and all (`ownership.md`,
  *Private paper*): a player's docket follows that player's paper rules.
- **Its papers lie inside red tape** (Bill, 2026-11-24: "red tape"): a red cord drawn under them,
  knotted at the label, round them and the label. The cord runs round the convex hull of their corners
  and the label's, pushed out by a radius, so each corner is an arc and the runs between are straight
  (Bill: "a smoothed polygon convex hull formed from the padded bounding box corners of the items",
  "rounded by a radius"). A docket with no papers has its tape round its label alone.
- **Each page draws the tape round the papers it reads**, so the tape's size never shows a note hidden
  from that page. **A paper's own readers decide who sees it**, not its docket's (Bill: "the paper
  itself, not the docket, determines visibility"): a public paper in a docket only the GM reads lies
  loose on the players' pages.

## Putting papers in and taking them out

- **Paper dropped inside the tape, or on the label, joins the docket**: a sticky, loose text or an
  image, and a character's card (below), never a docket ("doesn't join": no dockets inside dockets), a
  chip or an Influence card. **Touching the cord counts as inside** (Bill: "intersecting the line
  count[s] as 'inside the docket'").
- **Being in a docket is recorded**, as paper pinned to a card is: the paper remembers its docket and
  where it lies from the label, so every page agrees on who is in it whatever it can see, and the tape
  is drawn from that.
- **A card is more specific** (Bill: "card is more specific"): paper dropped on a card pins to the card,
  even inside a docket's tape, and leaves the docket. Where two dockets' tapes overlap, a drop joins the
  one on top.
- **A paper dragged within the tape just moves**, staying in, and the tape refits when it is let go.
  While it is carried the tape holds still as the other papers give it, and a paper let go still
  touching the tape stays, so the tape grows to take it: that is how a bundle gets bigger. **One dragged
  entirely clear comes off**, and the tape shrinks back to show it before it is let go.
- **Whoever reads a docket may add to it and take out**, as anyone who reads a note may move it; adding
  someone else's paper doesn't change who made it. No selecting several, no lasso.

## Characters in a docket

Bill (2026-11-25): "Let's allow characters to be in dockets", "This lets a docket manage several NPCs"
(`carves/fixes.md`, Item 12).

- **A character's card joins as paper does**: let go touching the tape or on the label, it puts its
  character in; dragged clear, the character comes out. **The whole character is in** ("putting the
  character in the docket means the whole thing is in there, but the table just shows the character
  being inside"): the tape is drawn round its card, while its pool mat lies where it is. A pool carried
  by itself brings its card, and where the card lands decides, as if the card had been let go there.
- **Whoever may move a character puts it in or takes it out**: its owner, anyone for an open one, and
  the GM, as long as they read the docket. The character's owner doesn't change.
- **A card in a docket shows as it does anywhere**: face-down to whoever may not read it.

## Carrying, tying, putting away

- **Only the label carries the whole docket** (Bill: "yes"): dragging it brings its papers, keeping
  where they lie, on every page, including papers the person carrying it can't read. There is no grip
  on the tape. **Its characters come too** ("card and pool and also anything attached to the
  character"): each card, its pool mat with what lies in its slots, and the paper pinned to the card,
  keeping where they lie. **Characters the carrier may not move follow anyway** (Bill, 2026-11-25), as
  their unread papers do: the page holding the game brings them to the label.
- **A docket can be tied up**, each page's own (Bill: "clicking the label piling the papers together and
  binding them? Just a visual trick -- not actually moving the papers around for the other players").
  A click on the knot at the label ties it and unties it. Tied, the papers it reads pile squarely under
  the label, a little offset like a real stack, with the red tape crossed over them in a bow; untied,
  the tape lies loose round them again. A character piles too ("Card and pool pile"): its pool mat, its
  card and the paper pinned to the card. **A tied docket is closed**: a drag anywhere on it carries the
  whole docket, and a paper is got at by untying it. A paper dropped on it joins at the place it was
  dropped, piled on this page and lying where it fell on the others. It stays tied through a reload,
  and others go on moving its papers meanwhile, which shows when it is untied. **No count** ("no count,
  the thickness says enough").
- **Filed or buried, a docket takes its papers with it**, and they come back with it laid out as they
  were (filing, `desk.md`; burying, `boothill.md`). Boot Hill and the drawer name it "Docket: " and its
  name. **Its characters go with it** ("Burying or filing the docket does so to the character, the pool
  mat, and any thing attached to it"), each with everything a character is buried with, into the
  Papers drawer on the GM's desk ("Papers drawer, all in"). A player's desk takes paper only, so the
  characters of a docket a player files are buried, as a character dropped there is.
- **A paper the page that puts it away can't read stays on the table**, loose where it lay (Bill:
  "yes"), as hidden notes are left alone on Boot Hill. So does a "just me" note on a docket another page
  puts away, and a character the one putting it away may not put away themselves, out of the docket now.
